Plumber in Mayberry Village, East Hartford, CT

Mayberry Village is a planned neighborhood in east central East Hartford, built in 1941 on the former Cannon family farm. Its mix of multi-unit buildings, single homes and duplexes means plumbing questions here often involve shared pipes and shared responsibility.

A planned neighborhood with two parts

Mayberry Village is roughly bounded by Burnside Avenue on the north, the Wilbur Cross Highway on the south and the Hockanum River on the east. The original upper section, once called Laurel Park Heights, is mostly multiple-unit construction. The later section, known as the New Village, has single and duplex homes.

In 1956 the units were offered for sale to residents. Many families bought their homes and rented out the other apartments, so some owners here are also landlords. The Dr. Franklin H. Mayberry School and East Hartford Middle School are landmarks in the area.

What shared buildings mean for plumbing

In a duplex or a multi-unit building, one set of pipes often serves several households. A leak under one sink can drip through a ceiling below. A clog in the stack can back up the lowest unit first, even when the blockage started upstairs.

That is why we ask a few questions before the visit. How many units are in the building, where is the shutoff for each, and who lives below the problem? Answering them lets us shut off the right line and avoid leaving a tenant without water.

Water near the Hockanum River and settling ground

A state-funded resilience report says the Mayberry Village area reportedly experiences flashy flooding associated with the Hockanum River. Town staff also told its authors that a few houses near the middle school are experiencing significant land subsidence.

We do not predict any single house will have a problem. We do suggest owners here know where their sump pump or floor drain is, test it before storms, and call early if a floor slab or a pipe entering the house seems to be moving.

Utilities and permits

MDC supplies East Hartford's water and sewer. For a building with several households the dividing line is worth knowing. The main in the street is MDC's, while the pipe running from the building out to that main belongs to the owner.

A landlord who is unsure where a blockage sits can phone MDC at (860) 278-7850 and press 1 before hiring anyone. A call to Inspections and Permits at (860) 291-7340 settles permit questions, and 811 covers any digging.
