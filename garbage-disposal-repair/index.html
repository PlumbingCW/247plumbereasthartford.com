The hum, the jam and the leak

A humming disposal with no grinding action is jammed. A spoon, a fruit pit or a bone has locked the flywheel. A plumber frees it with a hex key through the bottom of the unit, clears the debris and tests the motor. A silent disposal may have a tripped reset button or a dead motor.

A leak is a different story. Water at the top flange points to a failed sink seal. Water from the side or bottom can mean a cracked housing or a worn inlet gasket. The first is a repair. The second usually means a new disposal.

Before you call

These steps help us diagnose faster.

  • Switch it off at the wallNever put your hand into the disposal, even when it is off at the switch.
  • Press the reset buttonA red button on the bottom of the unit trips on overload. Press it once after the unit cools.
  • Look for the hex socketMost disposals have a hex socket in the bottom for a manual turn. A plumber carries the right key.
  • Check the dishwasherIf the dishwasher drain feeds the disposal, water standing in the sink may be coming from there.

Disposal clogs and the sink line

A disposal does not replace a drain pipe. Ground waste still has to travel through the trap and the wall line. Fats, coffee grounds and starchy waste collect in that line, which is why a kitchen sink that backs up often has a clog downstream of the disposal.

We check both. If the line is clogged, we clear it with a cable. Persistent kitchen clogs are covered on the drain cleaning page. Callers who also have trouble with the sink faucet can see faucet and sink repair.

East Hartford note: when the kitchen line is the symptom

If water from your sink backs into other fixtures, the problem may be bigger than the disposal. MDC states that the sewer lateral is the homeowner's responsibility and the main belongs to MDC. For widespread backups, the MDC Command Center is at (860) 278-7850, press 1.

Installing a new disposal involves an electrical connection. East Hartford's Department of Inspections and Permits at (860) 291-7340 can say whether a new circuit would need a permit.

Disposal drains connect through a trap and, with a dishwasher, an air gap or high loop as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) requires. Connecticut adopts the 2021 IPC through its 2022 State Building Code.
