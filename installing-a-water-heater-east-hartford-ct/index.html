Installing a Water Heater in East Hartford, CT: A Homeowner Guide

Installing a water heater in an East Hartford, CT home means more than swapping a tank. It involves shutoffs, a relief valve, venting or electrical work, a drain pan and a final safety check. This guide explains each step and where a licensed plumber matters most.

Know what you are replacing

Start by reading the label on the current heater. It lists the fuel, capacity, model and year. Match the fuel unless you plan a conversion, because switching from electric to gas or the reverse changes the whole job. Check the height and clearance, because a new tank may be taller than the old one.

Many replacements happen after a failure, so it helps to plan before an emergency. If you are comparing fuel and type, see water heater installation for how we approach the job.

The installation steps

Shut off and drain

Close the cold supply, turn off power or gas and drain the tank to a floor drain or outside.

Disconnect and remove

Disconnect water, gas or electrical, and vent. Remove the old tank, which can weigh more than people expect.

Set and connect

Set the new tank on a drain pan, connect water lines with new flex connectors and install a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe.

Gas, vent and power

Connect gas with a proper shutoff and test for leaks. Reconnect the vent with correct slope and clearances. Wire electric units on a correct circuit.

Fill, purge and test

Fill the tank with a hot tap open to purge air, restore power or gas and check for leaks. Set the temperature.

Temperature, safety and efficiency

Set the thermostat to a temperature that is safe for your household. Higher settings raise the scald risk, especially for children and older adults. A thermostatic mixing valve can lower the delivered temperature while keeping the tank hot.

If your home has a gas heater, place a carbon monoxide detector near the sleeping areas and test it. Combustion appliances need air and a clear vent. A plumber checks both after installation, and we explain the findings in plain language.

Choosing between tank types before you install

Standard tank heaters are the most common and the cheapest. Gas models recover faster than electric. Heat pump water heaters use less electricity but need space and air around them, plus a condensate drain. Tankless models save space and give continuous hot water but cost more and need larger gas or electrical capacity.

Think about where the heater sits. A unit in a closet needs adequate combustion air if it burns gas. A unit in a finished basement should have a pan and a drain. A unit in a garage needs protection from impact and, in some jurisdictions, a raised base. Your plumber will point out any of these that apply.

Finally, think about the warranty. Tank warranties usually depend on correct installation and on maintenance such as flushing. Keep the receipt and the model label photo in a safe place, because a warranty claim goes more smoothly with proof of when the heater was installed and by whom.

East Hartford note: permits, licensing and codes

In East Hartford, the Department of Inspections and Permits can tell you whether your replacement needs a permit. Call (860) 291-7340. Connecticut licenses plumbers at the state level and you can check a license at elicense.ct.gov. Plumbing work in the state follows the state building code, which adopts the International Plumbing Code.

Call before you dig if any part of your project involves the yard, such as a new gas line trench. Connecticut's 811 service marks utilities for free.

The 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), effective for permit applications filed on or after October 1, 2022. Ask the permit office which edition applies to your project.
