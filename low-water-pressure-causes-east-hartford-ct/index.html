Low Water Pressure Causes and Fixes in East Hartford, CT

Low water pressure in an East Hartford, CT home has several possible causes, from a clogged aerator to a leaking service line. This guide walks through simple checks first, then the problems a plumber should handle, so you find the cause instead of guessing.

One fixture or the whole house

The first question decides everything. If one faucet is weak, the problem is at that fixture. If every fixture is weak, the cause is upstream. Hot water only suggests the water heater. Cold water only suggests the cold supply or a shutoff.

Write down which fixtures are affected, when it started and whether neighbors have the same issue. That one page of notes saves a plumber time and helps you avoid unnecessary parts.

Checks you can do yourself

Clean the aerator

Unscrew the faucet tip and soak it in vinegar. Mineral scale is the most common cause at a single tap.

Open the shutoffs

Make sure the valves under the sink and the main valve are fully open.

Check the showerhead

Scale narrows the holes. Soak or replace it.

Test with a gauge

A hose-bib pressure gauge shows the actual pressure at the house.

The pressure regulator and the service line

Many homes have a pressure regulator near the main shutoff. It reduces street pressure to a safe level. When it fails, pressure can fall or spike. A failed regulator is a quick replacement. A gauge reading tells you whether it is doing its job.

The service line is the pipe that brings water from the street. Old galvanized pipe narrows inside, and a leak in the line reduces flow. See water line repair for what we do, and leak detection if you suspect a hidden leak inside the house.

Reading a pressure gauge

A pressure gauge screws onto an outdoor hose bib and shows the static pressure with water off. Take a reading with everything closed, then open a few fixtures and watch how far it drops. A large drop under load points to a restriction, and a steady low reading points to supply or regulator trouble.

Take readings at different times of day. Street pressure can fall in the evening when many neighbors use water, and a reading in the morning may look fine. Write down each reading, because a pattern helps a plumber decide whether the problem is the utility side or the house side.

If the reading is within a normal range and one fixture is still weak, the fixture is the culprit. If it is high, a regulator or an expansion tank may be needed. If it is low, the plumber will inspect the main valve, the regulator and the service line in that order.

East Hartford note: MDC water and your pressure

MDC supplies water in East Hartford. If pressure is low across your street, ask whether MDC is working on a main, since that explains temporary drops. MDC's line is (860) 278-7850. Mineral content also affects aerators and showerheads, and the annual Water Quality Report is available through MDC, with the lab reachable at extension 3901.

Before digging to inspect a service line, call 811 so utilities are marked. The East Hartford Department of Inspections and Permits at (860) 291-7340 can tell you whether repairs need a permit.

The International Plumbing Code (IPC), adopted in its 2021 edition by the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code, limits maximum static pressure in building supply systems and calls for a regulator where street pressure is high.

Older pipes and when repiping helps

Galvanized steel pipe corrodes inside and the opening shrinks over time. The result is steady pressure loss that gets worse year by year and rust-colored water on first use. Cleaning does not fix it, because the pipe is losing its inner diameter permanently.

If your house still has galvanized supply pipes, repiping restores flow. A plumber can cut a small test section to show the inside. Homes near Manchester and Glastonbury vary widely in age, so we judge each house on its own.

Do not ignore a pressure spike

Pressure that is too high is also a problem. It strains fixtures, shortens the life of appliances and can cause banging pipes. A gauge reading well above normal calls for a new regulator or an expansion tank on the water heater.

Check pressure once a year when you service other equipment. A ten-minute check can catch a slow failure early.
