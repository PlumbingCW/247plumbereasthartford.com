What Hockanum means in East Hartford

Historical accounts describe the land south of the Hockanum River as Hockanum and the land north of it as Podunk, although the names were never fixed. The town still lists the Hockanum River Trail and the Hockanum Linear Trail among its public paths.

Because no official boundary exists, we use the river as the anchor. If your home is south of the Hockanum River or within a short walk of the trail, this page is written for you. Mayberry Village, which has its own page, sits beside the river.

Why the river matters for a plumber

A Hockanum Sewer District was formed here in 1915, so public sewer service in this part of town has a long history. That fact does not tell us the condition of any single house, so we look at the actual pipes before recommending work.

The river matters most in wet seasons. A state-funded resilience report on the neighboring Mayberry Village area says that area reportedly experiences flashy flooding associated with the Hockanum River. For any basement near the river, that is a good reason to test the sump pump before heavy rain, not after.

A wet-weather basement checklist

These checks help whether or not you already have a pump.

  • Test the pump.Pour a bucket of water into the pit. The pump should start, empty the pit and shut off on its own.
  • Look at the discharge pipe.Water should leave the house and flow away from the foundation. A pipe that dumps next to the wall sends it straight back.
  • Check the power.Plug the pump into a working outlet and think about a battery backup if the power goes out in storms.
  • Know where the floor drain goes.A floor drain that backs up in a storm points to a sewer problem and not a pump problem.

Sewer, water and permits in the Hockanum area

East Hartford's water and sewer come from MDC. In a basement that matters for one reason. If the floor drain backs up, the blockage may sit in the public main or in your own lateral, and the two have different owners.

Phone the MDC Command Center at (860) 278-7850 and press 1 so crews can look at the main. If they find it clear, the repair is yours to arrange. Permit questions go to Inspections and Permits at (860) 291-7340, and any digging near the river bank or the trail should wait for an 811 call.
