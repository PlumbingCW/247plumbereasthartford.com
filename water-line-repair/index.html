Emergency Water Line Repair in East Hartford, CT

Emergency water line repair in East Hartford, CT deals with the pipe that brings water from the street into your home. Signs include a wet patch in the yard, a sudden drop in pressure, a meter that runs with every fixture off and a bill that jumps. Our licensed plumbers locate the leak, repair or replace the line and restore service, and the 24/7 line answers day or night.

If the line has burst, close the main valve inside the house. If the water is gushing in the yard, the curb stop belongs to the utility, so call us and MDC.

Signs the service line is leaking

A leak between the street and the house can hide for months. Look for a soggy patch or green strip in the lawn when it has not rained, the sound of water when everything is off, a meter that keeps moving and a bill that is higher than your use explains.

To test, turn off every fixture, then check the meter. If the dial moves, water is leaving the system somewhere. Closing the main valve inside the house and checking again tells you whether the leak is in the house or in the line outside. If the meter still moves with the main closed, the line is the likely culprit. See leak detection for pinpointing the spot.

Repair options

Spot repair

If the leak is at one joint or one short section, a plumber digs and repairs only that part.

Trenchless replacement

Pipe bursting or pulling a new line through the old path avoids trenching the full yard in suitable conditions.

Open trench replacement

The old line is dug out and a new pipe is laid. It can be faster for short runs and clear lawns.

Pressure regulator or valve work

Low or high pressure can come from a failing regulator rather than the pipe. We test it.

Line material and age

Service lines in older homes may be galvanized steel, copper or lead. Galvanized steel corrodes from the inside and restricts flow. Lead is a health concern. Copper is durable but can pit in aggressive water. Modern lines are typically copper, PEX or HDPE rated for water service.

Replacing the line when it fails is the moment to remove old materials. Callers who are also thinking about replacing interior pipes should read repiping.

East Hartford note: who owns which part

In East Hartford, MDC provides water service. Generally the utility owns the main and the portion to the curb stop, and the homeowner owns the line from the curb stop to the house. Call MDC at (860) 278-7850 to confirm the boundary for your address before digging.

Call 811 before any digging, which is the free Connecticut utility marking service. Repairs in the public right of way and some water line work may need a permit, so call the East Hartford permit office at (860) 291-7340.

Service piping from the meter to the house must use approved materials under the International Plumbing Code (IPC). The 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts the 2021 IPC, and the water utility sets its own rules at the meter.
