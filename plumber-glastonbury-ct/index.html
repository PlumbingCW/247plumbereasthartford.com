Why Glastonbury plumbing calls look different

Glastonbury is a large town with a mix of older properties and newer construction, and not every property connects to the public sewer. That changes what a plumber does first. A backed-up drain on a sewered street means checking the house line and the lateral. A slow drain on a septic property means looking at the tank and field too.

We tell you early which kind of problem you have. If the cause is a house drain line, drain cleaning and sewer line cleaning are the usual fixes.

Glastonbury official sources

The Town of Glastonbury owns and operates the public sanitary sewer system under its Water Pollution Control Authority, and service reaches certain areas of town. Sewer use billing is handled by the Department of Sanitation, Water Pollution Control Division.

The town Health Department covers septic systems and publishes a fact sheet on sewage backups, and the town GIS site can show septic system sketches and photos for a property. A building permit is required to construct or alter a building or structure under the state codes. Your water provider is named on your water bill, which is the quickest way to learn who to call about water quality.

Plumbing work in Glastonbury follows the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code, which adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Septic and sewer connections have separate local health and sewer rules.

Winter and well-kept homes

Glastonbury homeowners tend to invest in their houses, which is good for plumbing because problems are caught earlier. Even so, cold weather finds weak points. Pipes in exterior walls, outdoor spigots left connected to hoses and unheated garages are the usual failures. Disconnect hoses in the fall and open the spigot shutoff inside if your home has one.

If a pipe does freeze, keep the heat on, open the nearest faucet and call. A split pipe can release gallons per minute once it thaws, so we recommend closing the main valve first. After repair, we check the rest of the run for weak spots, because pipes that freeze once tend to freeze again at the same place.
