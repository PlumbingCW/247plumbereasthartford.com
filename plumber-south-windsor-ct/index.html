Plumbing for South Windsor homes

South Windsor is mostly single-family housing in neighborhoods built over several decades, with retail along Route 5 and near the Evergreen Walk area. Plumbing calls here often involve a basement: a water heater tank at the end of its life, a sump pump that failed, or a floor drain that backed up.

For hot water problems, see water heater repair and water heater replacement. If the basement floor drain overflows after heavy rain, sewer inspection helps find out whether the lateral is blocked or damaged.

South Windsor sewer facts

South Windsor's Water Pollution Control Authority oversees the town sewer system and approves connections and expansions. The town's wastewater treatment facility is on Vibert Road and processes an average of 2.5 million gallons per day. The system includes about 130 miles of sewer and 11 pump stations.

A town with many pump stations is a reminder that a sewer backup can have causes upstream of your house. If a neighbor has the same trouble, report it to the town. If only your house is affected, the blockage is more likely in your private lateral, and we can find it with a camera.

Plumbing work in South Windsor follows the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code, which adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Sewer connections also follow the town Water Pollution Control Authority rules.

Common South Windsor calls

Sewer lateral blockage

Roots and buildup in the line from the house to the street back up fixtures. A cable or jetting clears it.

Water heater failure

A leaking tank floods a basement. Shut off the cold supply and the power or gas, then call.

Basement bath leaks

Finished lower levels hide leaks until a stain appears. Leak detection finds the source without opening walls first.

Frozen pipes in outside walls

Cold snaps hit pipes on exterior walls and in garages. See burst pipe repair.

Planning ahead for a failing water heater

A water heater rarely fails without warning. Rust-colored water, a rumbling sound when it heats and moisture around the base all point toward the end of the tank. Replacing it on your schedule means you pick the size and type. Replacing it after a flood means a rushed decision.

Tankless units appeal to families who run showers back to back, but they need the right gas line and venting. Our tankless water heater page covers the trade-offs. When you call, tell us the current tank size and fuel, and we can tell you what a swap involves before the visit.
