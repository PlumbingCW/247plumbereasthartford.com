Toilet Clogged and the Snake Is Not Working in East Hartford, CT

If a snake will not clear your toilet in East Hartford, CT, the blockage may be an object or a main line problem. This guide explains why a snake fails, what to try next and when to call a plumber.

Do not keep flushing. Each flush can push more water onto the floor.

Why the snake may not work

A household drain snake, often called a closet auger when used for toilets, is designed to pass through the toilet trap. It can fail for several reasons. The clog may be a hard object such as a toy that the auger pushes around. The auger may be the wrong type, such as a coiled sink snake that scratches the porcelain and cannot pass the bend.

Or the blockage may lie farther down the line.

It also fails when the toilet is not the real problem. If a tub or sink also drains slowly, the blockage is in a shared drain, and working only on the toilet wastes time.

What to try

Stop flushing

Close the supply valve behind the toilet if the bowl is rising.

Try a flange plunger

A plunger with a flange seals the bowl outlet better than a flat cup.

Use a closet auger

Feed the auger through the bowl, turn the handle and pull back slowly.

Check other drains

Run the nearest sink. If it gurgles or backs up, stop and call.

When the toilet must come off

If an object is lodged in the toilet trap, the safest fix is often to remove the toilet, clear the trap from behind and reset the toilet with a new wax ring. A plumber can do this in a short visit. Forcing a tool can crack the porcelain, so it is better to stop than to risk the toilet.

If the blockage is in the main drain, a cable or hydro jetting clears it. Our toilet repair page covers fixes for flush problems and leaks, and drain cleaning explains the cable method.

Reading the situation before you escalate

Before calling, take a minute to check the whole house. Run the bathroom sink and the tub. Flush another toilet if you have one, and watch for gurgling. If everything else drains normally, the problem is local to the toilet. If other fixtures are slow, the blockage is farther down the line.

Also think about what happened. A child, a new product or a change in paper can point to the cause. A toilet that was fine until a wipe went down is different from a toilet that has clogged every week. Tell the plumber what you know, because the history shortens the diagnosis.

If you decide to pull the toilet yourself, shut the supply, flush to empty the tank and sponge out the rest, then unbolt it and lift it carefully. The bowl holds water and the toilet is heavy. Set a new wax ring on reassembly, and tighten the bolts evenly to avoid cracking the base. If this sounds like too much, a plumber can handle it quickly.

If the toilet is old, consider whether replacement makes more sense than another round of clearing. Older models with narrow passages clog more often, and a modern toilet with a smooth trapway can end the cycle. A plumber can compare the cost of a new toilet with the time you have already spent and recommend the better path for your bathroom.

Keep a plunger and a closet auger in the bathroom cabinet so you can act in the first minute of a clog, before the bowl fills. Early action prevents most overflows, and knowing when to stop prevents most cracked toilets.

East Hartford note: when it is the sewer main

The lateral from the house to the main is the homeowner's responsibility. If neighbors report the same problem, the main may be at fault. If only your house is affected, the blockage is likely in your private line.

Repairs to the lateral may need a permit. Ask the East Hartford Department of Inspections and Permits at (860) 291-7340.

A toilet reset needs a new seal and bolts set to the standard in the International Plumbing Code (IPC), adopted in its 2021 edition by the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code.
