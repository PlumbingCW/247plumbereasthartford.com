When a clogged drain becomes an emergency

A single slow sink can usually wait until morning. Water coming up through a floor drain, a toilet that gurgles when the washer drains, or a tub that fills with gray water when you flush are different. Those signs point to a blockage deeper in the line, and the next load of water has nowhere to go but back into the house.

Callers describe the same pattern again and again. The kitchen sink backs up first, then the basement floor drain starts to rise. If you see that sequence, stop using water everywhere in the house. Every gallon you add is a gallon the clog pushes back toward the lowest opening.

Our dispatchers ask four things: which fixtures are affected, how fast the water is rising, whether anything has been poured down the drain, and whether the house has a basement. Those answers decide whether a technician brings a hand auger, a drum machine for the main line, or a jetting setup. We also use them to tell you what to do until the truck arrives.

What the clog is telling you

One fixture is slow

The blockage is almost always in that fixture's own branch, such as hair in a tub trap or grease in the kitchen line. A cable through the trap arm usually clears it in one visit.

Several fixtures are slow

This points to the main drain or the house sewer. Roots, a collapsed section or a buildup of paper and wipes are the usual causes. The fix needs a longer cable or hydro jetting.

Gurgling and bubbling

Air is being pushed back through traps because water cannot move past a blockage. It often shows up in a nearby toilet when the washer or dishwasher drains.

Sewage smell or backup in the lowest drain

Treat it as urgent. The basement floor drain is the first outlet for a blocked main, and the water is not clean. Keep children and pets away from it.

Kitchen sinks: grease, food and what a cable cannot fix

Kitchen lines clog differently from bathroom lines. Fat cools inside the pipe wall and narrows it a little more with every dish load, until a bit of rice or coffee grounds finishes the job. A cable can punch through the plug, but the grease coating remains.

If the sink clogs again within a few weeks, the line needs a proper cleaning with a heavier blade or hot-water jetting, not another quick pass.

Garbage disposals make this worse because they push fibrous waste such as celery, potato peels and egg shells into a narrow trap. If a disposal sits on the clogged sink, the plumber will check the disposal outlet and the trap arm before cabling the wall line. East Hartford note: MDC owns the main, you own the lateral

In East Hartford the sewer main in the street belongs to the Metropolitan District Commission (MDC). The pipe that runs from your house to that main, called the lateral, is your responsibility. MDC says on its own sewer page that ownership and maintenance of the lateral is the homeowner's job.

That split matters during a backup. If every drain in the house is backing up, call the MDC Command Center at (860) 278-7850 and press 1, because the blockage may be in the main and MDC would clear it. MDC also states it will not assume liability if an owner or plumber works on the line before calling MDC first.

We make that call part of the first conversation so you do not pay to clear a pipe that was never yours.

Traps, vents and drain slope follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts in its 2021 edition. A good drain clearing leaves those parts exactly as the code expects.

What affects the cost of drain cleaning

Every clog is different, so we quote after we know where it sits. These are the factors that move the price.

Location of the clogA trap under one sink is a shorter job than a main line that needs a cleanout opened and a long cable run.
AccessA cleanout in the basement makes the work quick. If there is none, the plumber may need to pull a toilet or work through a roof vent.
MethodCabling costs less than hydro jetting. A camera look adds time but shows what is really in the pipe.
Time of dayNight and weekend dispatch is part of the service. You hear the price before work begins.
Repeat clogsIf roots or a broken section keep causing the same blockage, cleaning is a stopgap and repair is the real fix.

Homeowner policies often exclude the cost of clearing a clog itself but may cover resulting water damage, depending on the policy. Ask your insurer before you file.
