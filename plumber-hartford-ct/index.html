Older buildings, shared pipes

Hartford has a large stock of older housing, including two- and three-family buildings and apartment blocks. In those buildings one stack or one riser can serve several units, so a problem in one apartment can reach another. That is why a leak on an upper floor may show up as a stain two floors down.

When we arrive, we ask who has access to the shutoff and who is the owner or manager. If a burst pipe is flooding units, the fastest step is to close the nearest valve and call. See burst pipe repair and leak detection.

Hartford permit and utility contacts

The City of Hartford Department of Development Services, through its Licenses and Inspections team, issues permits and enforces codes. The city's main line is Hartford 311 at (860) 757-9311, and City Hall is at 550 Main Street.

MDC, the regional water and sewer authority, takes calls at (860) 278-7850, and its Command Center handles sewer backups. If several buildings on a street are affected, report it to MDC. If only your building is affected, the blockage is likely on your private side.

Plumbing work in Hartford follows the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code, which adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Multi-family buildings may also fall under city housing code enforcement.

Working in multi-unit buildings

A plumbing call in a multi-unit building has more moving parts than a call in a single house. Access, shutoffs and responsibility are shared. We ask who can authorize work, who has keys and whether other units are affected. If the problem is a common drain stack, clearing it may need access to a unit on a lower floor or the basement.

Landlords and property managers should keep a list of where each unit shutoff is, and tenants should know how to reach the manager at night. When those basics are in place, a burst pipe becomes a short repair rather than a flooded stairwell. For recurring stack problems, ask us about a camera inspection and jetting plan.
