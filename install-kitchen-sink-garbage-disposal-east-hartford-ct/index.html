How to Install a Kitchen Sink and Garbage Disposal in East Hartford, CT

Installing a kitchen sink and garbage disposal in an East Hartford, CT home involves a flange, a drain, a dishwasher hookup and wiring. This guide lists the parts, the steps and the points where a plumber makes the job safer.

Cut power at the breaker before touching any disposal wiring, and shut off the sink supply valves first.

Before you buy anything

Measure the sink cutout, the depth of the cabinet and the location of the drain. Check whether your cabinet has a dishwasher on one side, since the dishwasher drain hose connects to the disposal. Decide between a single and a double bowl, because the drain layout changes.

Choose a disposal sized to your use. A small household can do well with a smaller motor. A busy kitchen benefits from more power. Make sure there is an electrical outlet under the sink, ideally switched on the wall.

The installation sequence

Remove the old sink or disposal

Shut off supplies and power, disconnect the trap and remove the old unit.

Set the sink

Seal and clamp the sink into the countertop cutout.

Install the sink flange

Apply plumber's putty under the flange and secure the disposal mounting assembly.

Hang the disposal

Lift the unit into the mount and lock it. Knock out the dishwasher plug if a dishwasher connects.

Connect the drain and test

Attach the discharge tube to the trap, connect the dishwasher hose, restore power and run water to check for leaks.

Mistakes to avoid

A common error is skipping the dishwasher knockout plug, which leaves the dishwasher unable to drain. Another is over-tightening the mount, which cracks the sink or distorts the seal. A third is forgetting that a disposal needs a trap that slopes away from the wall.

Leaks at the flange are usually from too little putty or uneven tightening. Leaks at the discharge tube come from a worn gasket. If you replace a disposal and the sink still drains slowly, the clog may be in the line beyond the trap. See drain cleaning and garbage disposal repair for help.

Choosing a sink and a disposal that last

Sinks come in stainless steel, composite, fireclay and cast iron. Stainless is light and forgiving, though thin steel can dent and transmit noise. Composite and fireclay resist scratches but can be heavy and need a strong cabinet. Match the sink to the countertop and cabinet, and check the clearance for the faucet holes before you buy.

For disposals, look at motor power, grinding stages and noise insulation. Continuous feed units start with a wall switch and are common. Batch feed units run only when the stopper is in place, which some people prefer for safety. Check the warranty and whether replacement parts are easy to find.

Think about the trap and drain layout. A double bowl with a disposal on one side needs a continuous waste arm to connect the second bowl, and the trap must sit at the right height. A deep sink may need a longer tailpiece. A plumber can sketch the parts before you buy so nothing arrives that does not fit.

Finally, consider noise and use. A disposal does not handle everything, and starchy or fibrous waste is a classic cause of clogs. Run cold water while it works and for a short time afterward so the waste flushes through. Avoid grease, bones and coffee grounds in quantity, and keep fibrous peels out.

Finally, test everything before you close the cabinet. Run hot and cold water, fill the sink and let it drain, run the dishwasher through a short cycle and watch each joint with a dry paper towel held underneath. A slow drip shows up on paper long before it shows up on the cabinet floor, and catching it now saves a swollen base later.

If you rent, ask the landlord before swapping a sink or adding a disposal. Changes to plumbing and wiring in a rental can breach a lease, and the landlord may prefer to use their own plumber.

East Hartford note: permits, electric work and the sewer

A like-for-like swap may not need a permit, but adding a new circuit or moving plumbing might. Ask the East Hartford Department of Inspections and Permits at (860) 291-7340. Connecticut separates electrical and plumbing licenses, so a new outlet may need an electrician. License lookups are at elicense.ct.gov.

Grease sent down a disposal still ends up in the lateral. MDC says the lateral from your house to the main is the homeowner's responsibility. Keep grease out of the sink even if a disposal is installed.

The International Plumbing Code (IPC), adopted in its 2021 edition by the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code, governs the trap, the dishwasher drain connection and the vent for a kitchen sink and disposal.
