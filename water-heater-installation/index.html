Planning beats scrambling

A water heater installed on your schedule costs less stress than one installed after a failure. You can compare capacity, efficiency and fuel, check whether venting needs to change and line up an appointment when the family is home. The work is the same. The difference is choice.

Most planned installs fall into four groups. The first is a like-for-like swap on an aging tank. The second is a fuel change, such as moving from electric to gas or from gas to a heat pump unit. The third is a relocation, for example moving a heater out of a damp basement corner. The fourth is a new bathroom or addition that needs added hot water capacity.

Choices to make before install day

Capacity

Pick a tank size from household size, the number of bathrooms and whether tubs or high-flow showers are used at the same time.

Fuel

The existing gas line or electric circuit often decides this. Conversions are possible but add scope.

Venting

Atmospheric, power-vent and direct-vent gas heaters each need a different flue or pipe path.

Location

Heat pump water heaters need clearance and a drain. Tankless units mount on a wall and need an exhaust route.

East Hartford note: code, permits and the inspector

A new water heater is regulated plumbing work. The Town of East Hartford lists plumbing systems such as water heaters and gas lines among the work that usually needs a permit, and the Department of Inspections and Permits can be reached at (860) 291-7340. The town accepts online applications from residents, businesses and contractors.

Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code for this work. Licensed plumbers know the relief valve, discharge and venting requirements it sets. You can verify any contractor on the state eLicense lookup at elicense.ct.gov, where a P-1 license is the unlimited plumbing contractor class.

An installation has to satisfy the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which Connecticut adopts in its 2021 edition through the 2022 State Building Code. That includes the relief valve, expansion control where needed and a safe discharge path.

Heat pump and tankless: when they fit

Heat pump water heaters pull warmth from the surrounding air, so they work best in a space with room to breathe and a place for condensate to drain. A basement with plenty of volume suits one. A tiny closet does not.

Tankless units suit homes that want to free floor space or avoid standby losses. They need correct gas sizing and venting, and they benefit from regular descaling. Our tankless service page covers that side. If your current heater has simply failed, replacement has its own page with a same-day path.
