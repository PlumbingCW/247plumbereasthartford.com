What a sewer camera shows

The camera head sits on a flexible push rod with lights and a transmitter. As the rod moves down the line, the plumber watches the screen and records the run. Sections of pipe show up as continuous or broken, round or flattened, clean or coated.

Most findings fall into a short list. Roots look like gray hair crossing the pipe wall. A belly is a low spot where water sits and debris collects. An offset is a joint where two pipes no longer line up. A crack shows as a dark line, and a collapse shows as a narrowed opening.

When a camera is worth booking

Repeat clogs

If cleaning helps for a few weeks and then the drain slows again, the camera shows why.

Buying a home

A lateral can hide a problem that a visual inspection of the house cannot reveal. A recorded run lets you negotiate with facts.

Before a repair

Lining and spot repairs need an exact location and material. The camera provides both.

After a cleaning

A follow-up pass confirms the pipe is clear and shows whether roots or scale remain.

Locating the problem above ground

A camera with a transmitter lets the plumber pinpoint the depth and position of a defect from the surface with a receiver. That saves digging in the wrong place. The tech marks the location with paint or a flag and notes the depth on the report.

If the run stops short, the plumber records the distance and the reason. A hard stop may mean a collapse, a heavy root mass or a drop in the line. Each of those leads to a different plan, from sewer line cleaning to sewer line repair.

East Hartford note: reading the line with MDC in mind

The camera run usually ends at the connection to the sewer main in the street. That is the line between your responsibility and MDC's. MDC states that the homeowner owns and maintains the lateral and MDC handles the main.

If the recording shows a problem at or beyond that connection, tell MDC at (860) 278-7850. Having the video in hand helps the conversation. MDC also warns that it will not assume liability for work done before it is notified, so share the recording before any repair begins.

A camera inspection checks slope, joints and material against what the International Plumbing Code (IPC) expects of a building sewer. Connecticut adopts the 2021 IPC through its 2022 State Building Code.
