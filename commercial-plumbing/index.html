What commercial calls look like

Business plumbing problems arrive with deadlines. A restaurant cannot serve with a backed-up kitchen drain. A landlord has tenants calling about no water in the morning. A retail store with a floor drain overflowing has a floor to protect and customers to turn away.

We take the call, ask who has authority to approve work and what is at stake, and send a plumber. If you manage multiple buildings, tell us which one and who can give access, since a plumber who cannot enter wastes the trip.

Common commercial problems

Grease line backups

Restaurant kitchens produce fat that solidifies in lines and traps. Routine cleaning of grease traps and interceptors prevents most overflows.

Floor drain overflows

Floor drains in kitchens, laundries and mechanical rooms back up when the main line is blocked or a trap is dry.

Restroom stoppages

Heavy use clogs commercial toilets and urinals. A flush valve problem differs from a drain problem, and we diagnose which.

Water leaks and shutoff

Burst pipes in ceilings and wall cavities require fast isolation to protect finishes and stock. See burst pipe repair.

Grease traps, backflow testing and storm drains

Grease interceptors need regular pumping and cleaning. How often depends on the kitchen and local rules. Ask your building department or landlord which standard applies to your tenant space.

Backflow preventers protect the public water supply from contamination. Many commercial properties have them on fire lines, irrigation or process equipment, and they need periodic testing by a qualified tester. Which testing program applies is set by the water provider. In East Hartford that provider is MDC, so ask MDC what it requires for your service.

Storm drains and catch basins in parking lots collect debris and clog. Clearing them before the first heavy rain avoids ponding. If the basin ties to a private line, the property owner is usually responsible.

East Hartford note: the corridors we know

East Hartford's commercial life runs along corridors such as Main Street, Silver Lane and Burnside Avenue, with restaurants, shops, offices and multi-unit buildings sharing sewer and water service from MDC. Where several tenants share one lateral, a blockage in one space can back into another, so the first call should be to the property manager.

MDC states that the sewer main is its responsibility and the lateral from the building is the property owner's. Report main-line backups at (860) 278-7850, press 1, before commissioning outside work.

Grease interceptors, backflow preventers and floor drains in commercial space are addressed by the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Connecticut adopts the 2021 IPC through its 2022 State Building Code, and your building department says which parts apply.
