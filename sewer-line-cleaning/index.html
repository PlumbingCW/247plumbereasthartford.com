Emergency sewer line cleaning in East Hartford, CT clears the pipe that carries every drain in your house out to the street. When sewage backs into a basement shower or floor drain, the blockage sits in that line, and our 24/7 dispatchers send a licensed plumber with the right machine.

Until the plumber arrives, stop using water, keep the area ventilated and keep people and pets out of any standing wastewater.

Sewer backup: what to do first

A sewer backup shows up at the lowest drain. In most East Hartford homes with a basement, that is the floor drain, the basement bathroom or the laundry standpipe. Water that comes up there is carrying wastewater from the whole house, so treat it as contaminated.

Do not run the washer, dishwasher or shower while you wait. Every fixture upstairs feeds the same blocked line. If water is already on the floor, turn off power to the area only if you can reach the panel without stepping in the water, and call an electrician if you cannot.

Then make two calls. Report the backup to the MDC Command Center, and call us. The order matters, and the next section explains why.

Call MDC before anyone cleans the line

The Metropolitan District Commission runs the sewer mains in East Hartford. On its sewer backup page, MDC tells customers to call the Command Center at (860) 278-7850 and press 1 right away. If the blockage is in the main, MDC sends its sewer crew. If it is in the lateral, MDC explains that the lateral is the homeowner's responsibility and directs you to the next step.

MDC also says it will not assume liability when an owner or plumber does work before calling MDC. Our dispatchers tell every backup caller to report it first. It costs nothing, it can save you the cleaning bill, and it gives you a record of when the problem began.

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) sets expectations for building sewers, cleanouts and pipe slope. Connecticut adopts the 2021 IPC through its 2022 State Building Code, so slope and access matter when we judge a line.

What blocks a sewer line

Tree roots

Fine roots enter at joints and grow into a mat that catches paper. Older clay and cast iron laterals are the usual targets.

Grease and wipes

Fat hardens inside the pipe. Flushable wipes do not break down the way paper does and tangle with roots or rough spots.

Belly or offset

A low section holds standing water and debris settles there. A camera shows it clearly.

Collapsed section

When the pipe has failed, cleaning cannot fix it. The line needs sewer line repair.

How the cleaning is done

The plumber opens the cleanout, the capped fitting that gives direct access to the lateral. If there is no cleanout, access is made through a toilet flange or a roof vent, which takes longer.

A drum machine feeds a cable with a cutting head down the line. Blades cut roots and break up paper plugs. After the first pass the plumber runs the line again with a different head and flushes with water to confirm the flow. A camera look afterward shows whether the pipe is clean or whether roots, scale or damage remain.

If the cable keeps catching on something, the plumber stops and tells you. A line that snags the same place twice often has an offset joint or a break, and more cutting will not help. At that point hydro jetting or a repair plan is the sensible next step.
