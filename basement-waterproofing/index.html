Basement Waterproofing Plumbing in East Hartford, CT

Basement waterproofing plumbing in East Hartford, CT covers the plumbing side of a wet basement: sump pits, interior drains, floor drains, discharge lines and the backflow of sewer water. Our licensed plumbers assess where the water comes from and fix the drainage system that moves it out.

Find the water source first

A wet basement has four common sources. Water can enter through the floor from groundwater pressure, through walls from saturated soil, through a cracked foundation or from inside the house, such as a failed pipe or sewer backup. Each needs a different fix, and a plumber can tell you which you have.

A simple test helps. Tape a square of plastic wrap to the floor or wall. If moisture forms on the outside of the plastic, water is entering. If it forms on the inside, indoor humidity is the cause and a dehumidifier or ventilation may help.

What a plumber can do

Interior drain tile and sump

A channel along the footing collects groundwater and sends it to a sump pit. This is a plumbing and drainage job.

Floor drain service

A floor drain with a dry trap lets sewer gas in. A backed-up floor drain needs cleaning or a backwater valve.

Backwater valve

This valve lets sewage flow out but blocks it from flowing back in during a main-line backup.

Discharge and grading advice

Where the water goes after the pump matters. We check the discharge and point out grading or gutter issues.

What belongs to other trades

Not every wet basement is a plumbing problem. Foundation cracks, exterior grading, gutter downspouts and window wells are often a mason, excavator or general contractor issue. We tell you when the fix is outside plumbing so you do not pay for the wrong repair.

If the basement floods because the sewer backs up, the answer is different again. See sewer inspection to find the blockage or break, and drain cleaning for the cleanout.

Humidity, condensation and the things that mimic leaks

Cold water pipes sweat in summer and drip onto the floor. A dryer vented into the basement adds moisture. An unsealed sump pit lid lets groundwater vapor into the air. Each of these looks like a leak and none is solved by digging up the floor.

Before spending on drainage, we check pipes for condensation, look at the dryer vent and see whether the sump is covered. Insulating cold pipes and sealing the pit lid are inexpensive steps. If the basement is still damp after those, a drainage system is the next step, and we size it to the problem we actually found.

East Hartford note: rivers, brooks and permits

East Hartford borders the Connecticut River and is crossed by brooks, and low-lying properties can see higher groundwater in wet seasons. That makes a sump system and a working discharge more important there than on high ground.

Interior drain and sump work may need a permit. Ask the Department of Inspections and Permits at (860) 291-7340 before the work begins. For sewer backups, MDC takes reports of main-line problems at (860) 278-7850.

Floor drains, backwater valves and sump systems are plumbing items covered by the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Connecticut adopts the 2021 IPC through its 2022 State Building Code.
