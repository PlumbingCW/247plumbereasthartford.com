Established homes, established pipes

West Hartford is an older suburb with a long-settled housing stock, and many homes have decades-old plumbing. That means galvanized stubs, original shutoff valves and cast iron drain lines are still in service in some houses. A call about a drip can lead to a conversation about the age of the system.

We start by fixing the problem in front of us. If the pipe is worn out, we explain whether repiping makes sense. If the drain line is the issue, drain cleaning and sewer line repair are the next steps.

Drain lines in older neighborhoods

Older neighborhoods often have large mature trees, and tree roots are a common cause of sewer lateral blockages. The roots enter at joints, grow inside the pipe and catch debris until the line stops. A slow drain that returns every few months usually means roots or a belly in the line.

A camera inspection shows the cause. If roots are the problem, cutting or jetting clears the line, and a repair may be worth discussing if the pipe is damaged. See sewer inspection for how we look at the line before recommending work.
